Visualization 3 — Return Calendar

Bitcoin & Ethereum — Daily Return Calendar

A calendar heatmap of daily BTC and ETH returns from February 2025 to March 2026, where cell color encodes return direction and magnitude (green = up, red = down). Colored dots mark global events by category and colored cell borders indicate whale signal days, allowing pattern recognition across time at a glance.
Both
Interaction: Hover over bars to see the exact correlation value, event or transaction date, and associated price change percentage.
Takeaway

Both the ETH and BTC calendars show that days with positive and negative returns are mixed up throughout the period. This shows that crypto returns are very unpredictable from day to day, no matter what the macro conditions are. However, ETH shows a clear change in color density. The calendar goes from a mix of greens and reds in early 2025 to stretches that are mostly red from October onward. This visually confirms the line chart's sustained bear trend. BTC tells a different story. Stronger green clusters last longer into late 2025, which means that Bitcoin did better than Ethereum during the same time. There are only a few whale signal borders and global event dots, and they don't seem to cluster around red or green streaks. This suggests that neither force can reliably predict the direction of the next day's return on an individual day.

Design Choices

The choice of the calendar heatmap was motivated by the ability to maintain the inherent human perspective of time – days, weeks, months, which can reveal regimes changes and seasonality in data that could be obscured using a line plot. The green-red color palette reflects return value and direction encoded in the cell color. Instead of having event and whale signal plots separately, they are added to the calendar plot through dots and border coloring respectively, which saves space while not sacrificing the possibility of viewing all three plots together. Placing ETH and BTC side-by-side in two grid plots makes their comparison easier through identical scales.
