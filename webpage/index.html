Research Project

Global Events vs. Whale Wallets

An exploratory data analysis examining whether macro-level global events or the coordinated activity of large cryptocurrency holders ("whales") more strongly drives price volatility and market behavior.

Research Question: What impacts the crypto market more — global events or whale wallets?

📋 Project Introduction

Every year, it seems like investing in crypto is increasing exponentially, with Bitcoin reaching over $100,000 various different times at its peaks. Unlike traditional currencies, crypto is not controlled by a centralized government or backed by physical assets; instead, it is driven by a complex web of market forces. Our project focuses on the two potential major drivers of crypto prices, major global events and whale wallet activity. ‘Whale wallets’ are accounts that have large amounts of crypto that can potentially sway the market one way. Our analysis focuses on exploring the correlation strengths between the change in crypto prices on a given day and the potential drivers for that change through major global events or whale activity near that date. We aim to find out whether global events or whale wallet activities have a larger impact on crypto prices and potentially find out if any insider trading is present. Together, these layers paint a picture of a market shaped from major events and from above through rich investors. By visualizing both dimensions, we aim to tell a more complete story of what drives the crypto ecosystem than either perspective alone could offer.

📝 Summary & Findings

Across all five visualizations, a clear picture emerges. Global events and whale wallet activity are not separate forces competing for market influence; they interact, and the balance between them shifts based on market conditions. During the bull run from April to September 2025, whale accumulation was significant and moved in the same direction as price changes. This suggests that large holders actively supported the uptrend. However, the following bear market presented a different scenario. Whale buying continued into early 2026, but it could not stop the decline. Macro events, such as the Trade War Escalation and repeated Federal Reserve policy decisions, coincided with the sharpest price drops. The Top 5 comparison chart illustrates this imbalance clearly. Global events caused price swings of up to 9.3% within three days. In contrast, the Pearson r of -0.031 for whale transaction size indicates that raw transaction volume essentially has no predictive link to same-day price movement. The geographic bubble map further shows that market-moving events are mainly focused on the United States. These events are driven by regulatory decisions and Federal Reserve policy. Meanwhile, the daily return calendars demonstrate that neither whales nor events consistently predict direction on an individual day. Overall, the evidence suggests that global events create the macro conditions that ultimately guide market direction. Whale activity enhances movements within those conditions instead of initiating them independently. Future work could expand this analysis to higher-frequency intraday data to better capture the timing gap between whale transactions and price responses. It could also include a wider range of altcoins beyond BTC and ETH, and apply predictive modeling to quantify the relative impact of each factor on price changes across different market conditions.
